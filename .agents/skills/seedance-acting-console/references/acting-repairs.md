# Hypothetical acting repairs

These are constructed teaching examples, not observed generations or measured
quality gains. Compare the same identity, dialogue, motive and framing when
reviewing an authorized generation; the proposed repair predicts a clearer cue.

## Stiff close-up

**Brief:** A daughter asks her father to stay; contain the emotion in a close-up.

**Initial prompt:** “She is sad. Her brows rise, lips tremble, eyes dart, head
shakes, hands wring and shoulders collapse.”

**Hypothetical failure:** A mechanical sequence of expressions distracts from her
attempt to keep him listening.

**Smallest change:** Keep framing and dialogue. Replace the emotion routine with:
“She tries to hold his attention long enough to hear her out. Her gaze returns to
him when he looks toward the door; her reply catches once before she finishes.”

**Tradeoff:** Less overt distress; the intention relies on the partner's visible
response. Review whether the request to stay reads, not whether every facial
muscle appears.

## Same intention in a wide shot

**Brief:** The same daughter and dialogue, now seen full-body across a room.

**Initial prompt:** Reuse the close-up's subtle jaw and gaze cues.

**Hypothetical failure:** The cues are too small to carry the appeal. A body cue
can have the same problem: a rear hand closing against the thigh may be occluded
by the body or unreadable at this scale. Check the proposed camera side before
choosing an alternative; this is a prompt-level visibility risk, not evidence of
a rendered failure.

**Smallest change:** Preserve the goal and shot size: “She takes one step into his
path, stops at arm's length and leaves her hand open between them while asking
him to stay.”

**Tradeoff:** The appeal becomes spatial and more exposed. Do not add a close-up
or change the line to rescue facial detail the user did not request.

## Hidden face

**Brief:** A masked guard silently decides whether to let a friend pass.

**Initial prompt:** “His pupils soften and his mouth almost smiles.”

**Hypothetical failure:** The mask makes the proposed evidence invisible.

**Smallest change:** Keep the mask: “He tests whether the friend will wait; his
hand stays on the barrier, then releases it as his stance opens a narrow path.”

**Tradeoff:** The audience reads permission through action rather than knowing
his precise feeling. Preserve that ambiguity if it serves the brief.

## Eyes behind a veil

**Brief:** A veiled elder tests a young man; only her eyes show through a fine
mesh veil.

**Initial prompt:** "Her face shows contempt; her lips curl and her nostrils
flare."

**Hypothetical failure:** The mesh hides the mouth and nose, and the model may
lift the veil to show the requested expression.

**Smallest change:** Keep the veil: "Her eyes stay visible through the fine
mesh. She watches his hand, not his face; her gaze does not move when he
speaks, then narrows once when his fingers stop trembling."

**Tradeoff:** The menace depends on stillness and one change of gaze. Accept
less facial detail in exchange for mystery.

## Strike that jitters

**Brief:** The elder lunges to hold a needle at his neck, fast like a snake.

**Initial prompt:** "She moves quickly and aggressively toward him, grabbing
and threatening."

**Hypothetical failure:** Several fast verbs produce a smeared, jittery move
with no clear end position.

**Smallest change:** One burst with holds: "She sits coiled and still. Then she
lunges forward in one fast movement and stops dead with the needle a finger's
width from his neck. She holds there, motionless."

**Tradeoff:** One movement instead of a struggle. If the burst still smears,
keep the end position and cut to it in the edit.

# Screen Style Options

Style is a selected visual vocabulary, not a test of fighting effectiveness,
historical authenticity or character morality. These are optional screen-craft
interpretations, not martial-arts instruction or verified model controls.

## Selection

Keep a per-actor selection record. Offer choices only when useful:

| Selection | Optional screen emphasis | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No named style | The script's reaches, misses, pauses and reactions, without a school label | A hidden JKD default or technical training |
| Unspecified | Only supplied action facts; ask if a recognizable style matters | Permission to choose a style |
| JKD-inspired | An oblique silhouette, economical visible gestures, a brief interruption of an approaching action, uneven rhythm when requested | Mandatory interception, a particular lead side, low-kick rule, invulnerability or a victory |
| Wing Chun-inspired | A more frontal silhouette, compact arm shapes and close-range visual exchanges when requested | Mandatory chain punches, sustained pressure, a collapse or one universal stance |
| Custom or mixed | The caller's selected visual traits, separately assigned to each actor | A merged style for everyone, new abilities or copied performer identity |

Do not use a school name alone as an action description. Translate the chosen
look into observable posture, spacing and timing without a technique catalog.
Preserve explicit movement order when safe; keep a requested technical label
in planning metadata if useful, but do not turn it into an injury recipe.
If exact execution would require harmful detail, explain the boundary and
offer a non-instructional screen equivalent for approval.

A film reference or famous performer may indicate pacing or silhouette, not
permission to reproduce a face, voice or a source sequence. Separate those
questions and hand rights decisions to the caller. Avoid cultural, national,
gender and moral assumptions about who practices a style.

## Intensity Is More Than Speed

Record the intended viewer effect and the independent levers:

- Motion amplitude: small silhouette changes, a visible step or broader travel.
- Pace: sustained, stop/start or an isolated burst, with reaction time.
- Reaction: a check, a recoil, a stumble or a held aftermath, only as approved.
- Content ceiling: permitted contact appearance, injury continuity and any
  restrictions on distress or graphic detail.

Use `restrained`, `standard` or `heightened` as planning labels only when
helpful; encode the result in visible language. Higher pace does not authorize
more harm, blood, damage, sound effects or a different emotional performance.
Keep injury severity unchanged unless the caller explicitly authorizes a
revision. An uninjured character can remain frightened after a miss.

When intensity is materially missing, ask what the fight should feel like
and what contact/injury is allowed. A clearly labeled low-contact proposal is
acceptable for reversible planning, but is not an approved default.

## Performance And Medium

Derive reactions from supplied acting intentions. A character trying to
conceal fear may recover their posture after a missed reach; one trying to
end a confrontation may create space and hold. Neither implies rage or a
triumphant pose. Keep exact dialogue and its delivery window intact.

Retain the selected medium's motion behavior. Puppet articulation, cel holds
or stylized recoil may be intentional, not defects to repair into live action.
Do not add anime inserts, impact flashes, slow motion, camera shake, smash
cuts or sound layers as a fight preset. Return them as optional proposals
only when the caller asks for those choices.

## Fictional Contrast Exercise

Given the same approved non-contact interruption and mutual withdrawal:

- A selected JKD-inspired reading may use an oblique opening silhouette and
  a short gesture followed by a held separation.
- A selected Wing Chun-inspired reading may use a frontal silhouette and
  compact arm movement, ending in the same held separation.

Both retain the same outcome, no-contact ceiling, cast, dialogue and camera.
Neither needs a finisher. These are hypothetical alternatives, not empirical
comparisons or permission to replace a locked style.

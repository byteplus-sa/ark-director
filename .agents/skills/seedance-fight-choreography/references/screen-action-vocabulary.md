# Screen Action Vocabulary

Original fictional screen-direction examples, not copied technique lessons.
Use these to make a selected action legible, not to teach force application,
injury targets or real-world fighting. Preserve the script's named moves and
selected style when they can be described as screen action.

Read this reference when the caller needs concrete exchange wording rather
than only a state ledger. Supply stable actor IDs, world marks and camera side
first. Anatomy and frame-side placeholders below must be resolved from that
geometry, never guessed.

| Screen event | What to describe | What must remain readable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Straight punch, blocked or missed | Named arm travels in one direct visible line; partner's guard receives the apparent contact or the punch stops in clear space | Arm ownership, intended contact/gap, partner response and return position |
| Broad swing with avoidance | A's named arm traces a broad arc; B moves out of its visible path; A's motion settles | The swing and B's avoidance are connected, not two unrelated gestures |
| Compact hand exchange | One actor initiates; the other redirects the visible arm shape; separate the subsequent response into the next beat | Which arm belongs to whom; no fused hands or unexplained simultaneous hits |
| Scripted kick | Name the moving leg and visible path; show the selected block/miss and the return to a supported stance | Both the critical leg event and recovery fit the crop; no sliding support foot |
| Guarded interruption | An approaching gesture is visibly checked before it finishes; both actors register the stop | Interruption occurs after initiation, with a distinct held or released state |
| Clinch or grapple | Name the visible hold, body orientation and supported shift; preserve feet and clothing contact | Ownership of each arm and a continuous path into and out of the hold |
| Scripted stumble or fall | Establish the event that motivates it; show the loss of balance and the approved landing/end state | Ground contact, plausible continuity for the selected medium and no unapproved injury |
| Prop-blade clash or parry | Named prop paths meet in a visible staged crossing, then separate into supplied positions | Each prop remains with its owner; no fused blades, duplication or unexplained hand swap |
| Disengagement | One actor breaks the exchange and increases separation while the other stops or follows as scripted | The new distance and intention, not an automatic knockout or victory pose |

Sound, speed ramps and camera shake are separate choices. They cannot prove
contact that the shot never shows. For live performers, practical stunt
execution belongs to qualified professionals, not this prompt skill.

## Hypothetical three-beat exchange

Supplied brief: Two adult fictional rivals A and B, empty-handed, face one
another in a held full-body two-shot. A begins frame-left facing right; B
begins frame-right facing left. The camera sees A's right side and B's left.
A uses economical, JKD-inspired screen movement; B has no named style.
The approved action is one guarded punch, one missed swing, then mutual
withdrawal. No injuries, dialogue, winner, extra people or camera cuts.

1. A's right arm, visible on the camera-facing side, extends toward B's
   raised left forearm. Apparent contact stops at the guard. B's guard
   shifts back with the event; A draws the right arm back. Both retain
   their original screen sides.
2. B's left arm makes the scripted broad swing through the space between
   them. A moves back toward frame-left; the swing passes through a visible
   gap. B's arm settles without contact. A remains upright.
3. Both lower their guards slightly and step farther apart on their own
   sides. They remain standing and attentive to each other.

The caller supplies the shot's duration and exact frame-side wording from
the camera geometry. Allocate time for each initiating action, response and
recovery; do not claim this sequence fits any duration merely because it has
three beats. No technical pressure points, force mechanics or injury
optimization are needed to describe the approved screen event.

Return choreography and acceptance criteria, not a submitted video prompt
or evidence that a generated exchange has already succeeded.

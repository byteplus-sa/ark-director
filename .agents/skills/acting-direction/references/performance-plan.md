## Economical handoff

The analysis can be detailed; the generation prompt should not repeat the
entire analysis for every shot. Pass the relevant pursuit, trigger, response
and visible/audible evidence, once, to the encoder. Retain motive and source
interpretation in the scene record.

When action spans shots, carry emotional and physical state across the cut.
Do not restart the character at neutral in every clip. A listener can register
an event while the speaker remains in frame; coverage need not insert a
reaction close-up. A held wide may be exactly the right shot.

Quiet or observational work need not contain a tactic switch, confrontation,
reversal or facial change. Record the intended sustained attention, non-action
or ambiguity and how the selected framing preserves it.

## Hypothetical example

Supplied scene: Two adult siblings fold a tablecloth after dinner. A is leaving.
B wants A to stay long enough to finish their conversation. A picks up a bag
while B says the locked line, "There is one corner left." Keep a held medium
two-shot and the requested language; no tears or additional dialogue.

- B's tactic is to delay the departure through the shared task, not to "look sad".
- The trigger is A taking the bag, not an arbitrary timestamp.
- B registers the departure by looking from the unfinished fold to A, then
  offers the remaining corner while speaking the exact line.
- A must decide whether to take that corner; the response belongs to A's
  established intention. If the supplied ending is missing, ask rather than
  inventing reconciliation.
- Acceptance: the bag action precedes B's appeal, the cloth remains the
  physical channel, and the shot leaves room to read A's response.
- Failure: both people stare neutrally through the line, or the edit ends
  before the intended response. A quiet response is not itself a failure.

This predicts a more legible attempt; it is not a measured generation result.

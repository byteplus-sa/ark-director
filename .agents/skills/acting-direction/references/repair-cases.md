# Acting Repair Cases

These are hypothetical diagnoses. Keep the user's locks and choose the
smallest repair supported by actual evidence.

| Symptom | Check first | Bounded repair |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speaker emotes but does not seem to want anything | Does the script establish a goal and a partner? | Name the attempt and what response the speaker is testing |
| Listener waits blankly for their line | Is listening direction present and readable? | Give the listener a decision or an intentional refusal to respond |
| Every line has the same intensity | Are materially different triggers treated identically? | Distinguish the attempts and the information registered; do not simply increase amplitude |
| Nervous gestures repeat mechanically | Were cues selected from an emotion list instead of the task? | Remove unrelated gestures and retain the physical channel of the pursuit |
| Supposed revelation has no effect | Is it new information to this character at this moment? | Correct the knowledge/trigger mapping before directing a reaction |
| Quiet wide is judged flat | Is the intent visible through spacing, posture, action or sustained attention? | Preserve the wide and restraint; choose readable evidence, not mandatory close-ups |
| Good take feels lifeless in the cut | Was the registration, held reply or response trimmed away? | Propose restoring the relevant duration; do not regenerate automatically |
| Dialogue is correct but unconvincing | Has someone listened for pacing, emphasis and the partner-directed intention? | Review delivery separately from transcription; preserve locked words |

## Ambiguous opposing intentions

Supplied scene: One character wants a confession; the other wants to end the
conversation. Do not claim they share a goal of "keeping things comfortable"
unless the writing supports that reading. A tactic-first analysis can preserve
their conflicting goals while naming the common event: the attempt to obtain
an answer. Neither character plays the audience's eventual discovery.

## Partial evidence

If only the submitted prompt is available, state which performance decisions
are absent or ambiguous. Do not report frozen eyes, flat speech, a missing
reaction or bad timing as observed. Request the actual take and relevant
scene context for those judgments. When only a cut is available, distinguish
visible failure from an unverified theory about the original generation.

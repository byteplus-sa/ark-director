# Outfit prompt patterns

Load this reference when the outfit comes from photos, or includes glasses, a
hat, jewelry or footwear.

## Garment and outfit photos

A photo of a person wearing the target clothes supplies **clothing only**.
Bind and fence it in the References block, then restate the binding in Subject.

```text
References:
@Image 1: approved character sheet — identity authority and layout authority.
@Image 2: photo of the target outfit — garments only: cut, color, fabric, fit.
From @Image 2 use clothing only. Its face, hair, body, pose, expression,
background and lighting are not used.
```

Subject line: `Same person as @Image 1; wear the [top], [bottom] and [shoes]
from @Image 2.` If two photos show different items, give each its own role:
`@Image 2: the jacket. @Image 3: the trousers and shoes.`

When a garment photo is a flat lay or mannequin, say so and ask for the garment
worn naturally on the person's build from `@Image 1`, not the mannequin's.

## Outfit from text

Write item by item, from inner to outer layer, then footwear.

```text
Outfit: a fitted ivory silk blouse with a small collar, sleeves to the wrist;
a charcoal wool blazer open at the front, sleeves to the wrist; high-waisted
charcoal trousers to the ankle; tan leather loafers.
```

State sleeve and hem lengths so the back and front panels agree. Name colors in
plain words. Keep fabric words that show on camera: matte, ribbed, woven, denim.

## Glasses

Part of the outfit, listed with the garments.

```text
Round thin gold-framed glasses with clear non-reflective lenses and eyes fully
visible through them, worn in all three panels.
```

The close-up panel now shows the glasses on the face anchor. Record in
`character.md` that this variant's close-up differs from the bare-face parent.

## Hats and headwear

State what the hat covers and what hair shows, in the back view as well.

```text
A navy baseball cap worn forward; the cap covers the crown, and the same
[hair length and color from character.md] shows below the cap at the nape and
sides. The face is fully visible under the brim.
```

A brim must not shade the eyes in the close-up. If it does, ask for the brim
raised slightly. If the hat hides identity-critical hair, tell the caller the
hair anchor is weaker in this variant.

## Jewelry and watches

Name the exact pieces and the side they sit on, as seen in the frame and in the
character's own terms. Repeat them in the back and front panels where visible.
Keep the parent's always-worn pieces unless the brief removes them; if so, list
them under REMOVE.

## Footwear

Footwear is part of the outfit and shows in both body panels. Name type, color
and height. Floor contact is plain gray studio floor with a faint contact
shadow, as in the parent.

## Exposure and body marks

A lower neckline, short sleeve or skirt can show skin the parent hid. Describe
the visible skin as the same tone as the face. List any mole, scar or tattoo in
PRESERVE with its position as seen in the frame. If the parent never showed the
area, do not invent a mark.

## Negative line

Extend the parent's Negative list with: `no original outfit, no old clothing
carried over, no face change, no hairstyle change, no held objects, no props in
hands`.

# Observed Failures

Read [the entrypoint](../SKILL.md). These are failure patterns generalized from
reviewed native-audio outputs of English-dominant Taglish takes, not a
per-line log and not hypothetical cases. Use them to anticipate risk and to
choose what to check in listening. They do not prove a word is unsuitable, and
they do not establish the correct pronunciation.

Evidence level: speech-to-text transcripts plus model-based listening
(`seed_audio_understand`). No native-speaker confirmation. Transcripts are the
evidence for the heard words; the model-based listening cannot judge accent or
stress, so any "correct" verdict is provisional until a listener confirms it.

## Patterns

- Whispered and shouted positions produced most errors. Normal-volume,
  mid-sentence placement produced the fewest.
- Word-initial Tagalog after a pause was garbled more than the same word
  inside a sentence.
- A long, plot-critical word in a shouted opening position was the hardest to
  repair; a pronunciation note did not reliably fix it, and a repetition of the
  word mid-line by another speaker came out closer.
- An English look-alike replacing a Filipino interjection or address term (an
  English greeting heard for a short exclamation) responded to one targeted note
  naming the intended word.
- Contractions such as *'ng* may be normalized to a fuller spoken form with the
  meaning intact. Decide whether that variant is acceptable before spending a
  retake.

## Using these patterns

1. For a locked line containing a pattern above, flag it as a listening
   priority in the review plan. It is not a reason to rewrite without scope.
2. If rewriting is in scope, propose moving the critical word mid-sentence at
   normal volume, as a draft for the user to accept.
3. After a new take, record the locked text, what was heard, the note tried and
   the result in the project's own review notes, not in this skill.
